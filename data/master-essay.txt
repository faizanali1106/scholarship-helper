Most people my age are just beginning to take responsibility for their daily decisions. I've been making life-impacting calculations for years.

Living with Type 1 diabetes means there are no off days. Every meal, every activity, and even stress requires me to think ahead, calculate, and act, especially as a student athlete. I've learned to manage variables like carbohydrate intake, insulin timing, and physical activity in a way that demands both precision and consistency. It's a responsibility that has shaped how I approach everything else in life.

This constant need for awareness has built discipline in me that goes beyond academics. While many students are learning time management for the first time, I've had to develop it out of necessity. Missing an assignment has consequences, but mismanaging my health has immediate effects. That perspective has made me more focused, more accountable, and more prepared for independence than most of my peers.

It has also influenced my decision to pursue a degree in marketing. At its core, marketing is about understanding behavior—how people think, what drives their decisions, and how systems influence outcomes. Living with diabetes has trained me to think in a similar way. I am constantly analyzing patterns, adjusting strategies, and making informed decisions based on changing conditions. I want to apply that same analytical mindset to understanding and influencing consumer behavior.

Managing a chronic condition comes with ongoing medical costs that extend beyond what most families have to consider. Despite these challenges, I am committed to continuing my education and building a future where I can apply my skills in a meaningful way.

Type 1 diabetes is not something I can set aside—it is a constant part of my life. But instead of allowing it to limit me, I have used it to develop discipline, resilience, and a strong sense of responsibility. These qualities will continue to guide me through college and into my career.

Thank you for your time.

Robert "Luke" Peach
